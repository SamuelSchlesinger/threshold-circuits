ABSTRACT (LATEX)
We interpret threshold circuits through their activation transcripts, adaptive
scalar comparisons, finite-state bias vectors, and additive input summaries.
These representations support lower bounds, exact normalizations, and counting
algorithms, with distinct hypotheses for each conclusion.

For arbitrary-depth, arbitrary-real-weight circuits computing inner product
modulo two on $m+m$ bits, a leaf-count rectangle theorem gives
$\sum_{j=0}^{e}\binom qj\ge2^{m/2}$, where $q$ counts gates reading original
inputs and $e$ is their worst-case activity. Under the additional restriction
that only one party's input-weight vectors are collinear, we prove a
distribution-sensitive result: the corresponding transcript has conditional
entropy greater than $m-2$. With randomized error at most $\epsilon<1/2$, its
conditional entropy is at least $(1-2\epsilon)m-4\log_2m-14$; the leading
coefficient is optimal for the randomized model. Polynomially many
input-reading gates therefore require $\Omega(m/\log m)$ average activity in
this restricted model.

For a fixed scalar input direction, we characterize the exact query--activity
capacity by the Hamming-ball size $B(q,e)$. A polynomial-size
enumerative-coding construction realizes any threshold circuit using $N+1$
collinear input-reading gates with activity at most $\lceil N/2\rceil$.
Combining this construction with a prior all-splits rectangle-free family
gives an asymptotically tight scalar resource curve. Hyperplane cuttings yield
exponential gate lower bounds for bounded input rank and bounded predecessor
fan-in, allowing arbitrarily many directions within the rank bound.

Finally, integer zonotopes bound the number of additive summaries by
$(\mathrm{e} NW/\sqrt r)^r$, giving an exact $\#\mathrm{SAT}$ algorithm for
rational circuits whose input rows lie in a supplied bounded-integer rank-$r$
span. At $r\sim N/\log_2N$ and $W=N^{o(1)}$, the exponential factor is
$2^{N/2+o(N)}$. Totally unimodular summaries yield an exact matroid state
count; totally unimodular internal cuts yield Fibonacci-size linear-query
programs. Polyhedral, integer-programming, and quadratic unique-sink
interpretations complete the representation map. No unrestricted superlinear
total-gate lower bound is established.
